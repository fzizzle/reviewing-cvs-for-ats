# Feedback rubric

Lead with the 3–5 highest-impact issues, then list the rest. For each: what, where (quote it) and the fix.

| Area | Look for | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Target fit | Top third doesn't say what they want or why they fit | Tagline = target role (and availability for internships); strongest target-relevant evidence first |
| Density | Body text under 10pt printed (`cvats layout`); bullets over 2 lines; more than 5 bullets on a role; module-by-module grades; generic soft-skill lists | Cut to 3–5 bullets on recent roles and 1–2 on old ones; keep top grades only; bigger text, not smaller |
| Repetition | Profile restating body facts; the same fact twice; bullets sharing an opener; "Forecasting: forecast…" | Profile = goal + value only; each fact once; vary verbs (`cvats repeats`) |
| Fidelity | Verbs stronger than the source ("helped" → "built", "worked with" → "led"); rounded ranges ("15–20" → "20"); scope creep ("firm-wide") | Keep the source's strength unless the person confirms more |
| Evidence | Duties without outcomes | Add numbers the person confirms: size, time, money, %, volume |
| Jargon | Internal tool or team names outsiders can't read | Explain in plain words, keep the name in brackets |
| Order | Old or irrelevant roles above relevant ones; students without current studies first | Reverse-chronological within sections; current study first for students |
| Contact | No city; phone without an international prefix when applying abroad; no LinkedIn | "City, Country · +CC number · email · LinkedIn" |
| Languages | "Intermediate" in European markets | CEFR level, and say if it's self-assessed |

Close every feedback message with open questions: facts that only the person can confirm.
